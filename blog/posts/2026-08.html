AUG 2026 — things I hate (while using it daily)

5 things I hate about AI in dev

I use AI every day. that's exactly why these bother me — not because it fails, but because of how it quietly reshapes the way I think while coding.

  1. syntax Alzheimer. it starts with autocompletion and creeps further. the syntax is still there, but the reflex to reach for it first fades if you're not deliberate about keeping it sharp.
  2. prompt Bermuda. you start with a simple question. a few prompts later you're somewhere else entirely, and the original problem is gone. staying on track is on you, not the model.
  3. confidently wrong. it sounds certain even when it isn't. so you double-check everything — and sometimes verifying costs more than just thinking it through first.
  4. the buddy that oversteps. depending on the model, it pushes a direction you didn't ask for. helpful when you catch it, expensive when you don't. you're constantly steering.
  5. it's a reflex, and that's the trap. the real issue isn't usage, it's control: asking before thinking. cutting it out isn't the fix — staying intentional is.

AI didn't replace development. it shifted where the cognitive load is.
